What Is a Yen Stablecoin (JPY Stablecoin)? JPYC and Japan's Stablecoin Rules

A yen stablecoin is a digital token designed to keep a steady value of one Japanese yen per token, so that it can be exchanged for yen at a fixed one-to-one rate. One example in Japan is JPYC, which JPYC Inc. began issuing in October 2025. Under Japan's Payment Services Act, a stablecoin tied to the yen and issued in line with the law's requirements is an "electronic payment instrument", a category separate from crypto assets such as Bitcoin. This guide explains what JPYC is, who may issue yen stablecoins, how they differ from USDC and USDT, and where STBLpay, which is under development, fits.

Updated: November 10, 2026　By Ango-ya LLC

What is a yen stablecoin?

A stablecoin is a digital token designed to hold a steady value, usually tied one-to-one to a legal currency. It moves over a blockchain, a shared record of transactions that is hard to change after the fact. A yen stablecoin is one tied to the Japanese yen. You may also see it called a JPY stablecoin, a Japanese yen stablecoin or a yen-pegged stablecoin. These names all describe the same kind of token.

Not every token that follows the yen is treated the same way by the law. Materials from Japan's Financial Services Agency (FSA) split stablecoins into two groups. The first group is issued at a price tied to a legal currency (for example, 1 coin = 1 yen) and promises redemption at the issue price. It is regulated as digital money, meaning a means of remittance and payment. The second group is everything else, such as coins that try to keep a steady value by algorithm. It is regulated as crypto assets or financial products[3].

JPYC is a yen stablecoin issued by JPYC Inc. (JPYC株式会社). Here are the facts as published by the issuer and the Financial Services Agency.

How does Japan regulate stablecoins?

Stablecoin regulation in Japan sits mainly in the Payment Services Act (資金決済に関する法律). The FSA states that from June 1, 2023, a new system took effect for businesses that act as intermediaries for stablecoins linked to a legal currency. Operating such a business in Japan requires registration under the Payment Services Act or the Banking Act[1].

The Act defines an "electronic payment instrument" (Article 2, paragraph 5). Broadly, it is a currency-denominated asset, recorded electronically, that can be used to pay unspecified persons for goods or services, can be bought and sold with unspecified persons, and can be transferred electronically. A "specified trust beneficiary right", a form of trust beneficiary right, is also listed[2]. A currency-denominated asset is one expressed in, or to be repaid in, Japanese or foreign currency (paragraph 7)[2]. The Act's definition of crypto assets excludes electronic payment instruments (paragraph 14), so the two are separate categories[2]. Two rules matter most.

  • Issuing. The FSA's materials show that issuers are banks and funds transfer service providers, plus trust companies that use trust beneficiary rights[3].
  • Dealing. Buying, selling or exchanging electronic payment instruments as a business, brokering such deals, or managing them for others requires registration with the Prime Minister as an "electronic payment instruments service provider" (電子決済手段等取引業者)[2]. The FSA publishes a list of registered providers[1]. Since June 1, 2026, a business that only brokers such deals on behalf of a registered provider can instead register as an "electronic payment instruments and crypto-asset service intermediary" (電子決済手段・暗号資産サービス仲介業)[9].

Which rules apply to a given service depends on how the service is designed. This page describes what the law and regulator say. It is not legal advice.

What other yen stablecoins exist or are planned?

Outside Japan, GYEN is a yen stablecoin issued by GMO-Z.com Trust Company, Inc., which is chartered as a limited purpose trust company by the New York State Department of Financial Services. The issuer states that GYEN is always 1:1 redeemable with Japanese yen[10]. We have not assessed how Japan's Payment Services Act applies to GYEN.

Banks in Japan are also testing a jointly issued yen stablecoin, and we list only what has been officially announced. On October 16, 2026, MUFG Bank, Mizuho Bank, Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation, Mitsubishi UFJ Trust and Banking Corporation and TISI Inc. announced a proof-of-concept of jointly issuing a yen stablecoin. It ran from July 17 to 23, 2026. The stablecoin was issued as a specified trust beneficiary right under a trust contract, with the three banks as co-settlors and the trust bank as trustee[6].

The announcement says the design is the one assumed at this point and may change, and that the participants will keep studying how to put it into practice[6]. A proof-of-concept is a test, not a launch, so treat it as a sign of direction rather than a product you can use today.

How is a yen stablecoin different from USDC or USDT?

The main difference is the currency the token is tied to. Each issuer also describes its own backing in its own words.

JPYC compared with USDC and USDT
Item JPYC USDC USDT
Tied to Japanese yen[4] US dollar[7] US dollar[8]
Issuer JPYC Inc.[4] Circle[7] Tether[8]
Value, as the issuer states it Exchangeable with the yen at 1:1[4] Redeemable 1:1 for US dollars[7] Pegged 1-to-1 to a matching currency (1 USD₮ = 1 USD)[8]
Backing, as the issuer states it Yen deposits and government bonds[4] Highly liquid cash and cash-equivalent assets[7] Tether's reserves[8]

The exchange rate between the yen and the US dollar changes over time, so the value of a dollar token is not fixed against the yen. A shop that sets prices in yen can keep price and payment in the same currency with a yen stablecoin. See stablecoin payments in Japan for how a shop can accept one. Under the Payment Services Act, a currency-denominated asset can be in yen or in a foreign currency[2], so the law's categories are not limited to yen tokens. We have not assessed how the law applies to USDC or USDT specifically.

How does STBLpay fit?

STBLpay (すてぶるペイ) is a payment service in which buyers pay in yen and merchants receive JPYC. It is under development, and the launch date has not been set (as of November 2026).

  • The buyer pays by Japanese-yen bank transfer and approves the payment with a passkey (face or fingerprint). If the buyer's JPYC balance is enough, no bank transfer is needed. Buyers need a JPYC EX account, which is available to individuals residing in Japan, so STBLpay is for selling to buyers in Japan.
  • The merchant receives sales in JPYC. The service does not rely on card companies' credit screening. STBLpay connects the merchant's and the buyer's wallets. It is a permissionless design with no step where the provider approves each merchant, so there is no card-style prior merchant screening.
  • The merchant withdraws to yen from its own JPYC EX account, which is available to individuals residing in Japan and Japanese corporations.
  • The provider, Ango-ya LLC, does not hold buyers' or merchants' funds or private keys (non-custodial).
  • The fee is under consideration. A single flat rate for all merchants is planned, and nothing is added on top for the buyer.
  • Use is subject to the terms of service, in which the merchant represents that its business is not illegal under Japanese law, and to legal requirements.

Frequently asked questions

What is a yen stablecoin?

A yen stablecoin is a digital token designed to keep a steady value of one Japanese yen per token. It moves over a blockchain and is meant to be exchangeable for yen at a fixed one-to-one rate. JPYC, issued by JPYC Inc., is one example in Japan.

Is JPYC a cryptocurrency?

Under Japanese law, not in the usual sense. JPYC Inc. issues JPYC as an electronic payment instrument under the Payment Services Act, and the Act's definition of crypto assets excludes electronic payment instruments. Whether a given yen-linked token falls into this category depends on how it is designed.

Who can issue a yen stablecoin in Japan?

Financial Services Agency materials show that issuers of stablecoins treated as electronic payment instruments are banks and funds transfer service providers, plus trust companies that issue them as trust beneficiary rights. JPYC Inc. issues JPYC as a registered funds transfer service provider.

Is JPYC backed by real yen?

JPYC Inc. states that the reserve assets for JPYC, which are yen deposits and government bonds, are held in an amount equal to or more than the outstanding balance, and that JPYC can be exchanged for yen at 1 to 1. This is the issuer's own description, so read the issuer's current materials before relying on it.

How is a yen stablecoin different from USDC or USDT?

The main difference is the currency. A yen stablecoin such as JPYC is tied to the Japanese yen, while USDC and USDT are tied to the US dollar. Their issuers also differ: JPYC Inc., Circle and Tether each describe their own reserves.

Does STBLpay use a yen stablecoin?

Yes. STBLpay is a payment service in which buyers pay in yen and merchants receive JPYC. It is under development, and the launch date has not been set (as of November 2026).

Can people outside Japan get JPYC?

Getting JPYC from the issuer requires registering on JPYC EX. At launch, the issuer verified identity only with the My Number card's public personal authentication (JPKI). This guide has not checked JPYC Inc.'s current terms on who may register, so check them before you rely on this.
