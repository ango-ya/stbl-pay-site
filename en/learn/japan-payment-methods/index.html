Payment Methods in Japan: How Customers in Japan Pay Online

The main payment methods in Japan online are credit cards, convenience-store (konbini) payment, bank transfer, cash on delivery, carrier billing, QR code and smartphone payments, and pay-later ("atobarai") services. Credit cards carry most of the cashless spending: for 2025, Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) counted 134.6 trillion yen of credit card payments out of 162.7 trillion yen of cashless payments[1]. Which methods an overseas shop can offer depends on its payment gateway or processor, and some methods need a business located in Japan.

Updated: November 9, 2026　By Ango-ya LLC

What are the main payment methods in Japan?

METI calculates how much consumers pay by cashless means each year. For 2025 it reports 162.7 trillion yen in total, split into credit cards (134.6 trillion yen), code payments (16.6 trillion yen), electronic money (6.0 trillion yen) and debit cards (5.5 trillion yen)[1]. These figures are for consumer payments overall, not for online shopping alone.

This guide does not give a market share for each method. Instead, the next section describes each method as its operator or a payment provider describes it.

What are the Japan payment options, and what should a merchant know about each?

Terms differ by provider and contract, so treat the right-hand column as points to check, not as rules.

Credit and debit cards The buyer enters card details at checkout[2]. Stripe lists Visa and Mastercard for accounts in all countries, and Japan Credit Bureau (JCB) for a set of countries that includes the United States[2]. KOMOJU's local card acquiring in Japan covers Visa, Mastercard, American Express and JCB, and the merchant must be registered in Japan to use it[4].

Bank transfer The buyer sends money from an ATM or online banking[4]. KOMOJU lists no upper limit for domestic bank transfer[4]. In Stripe's documentation, yen bank transfers are for Stripe accounts with a business location in Japan, and cross-border payments are not supported[5].
Cash on delivery At delivery, the carrier collects the amount the sender specified from the recipient[6]. Japan Post's domestic service sends the collected amount to an account at a financial institution in Japan[6]. Japan Post says it does not provide cash on delivery for international mail[7].
Carrier billing Docomo describes a service in which purchases made on a smartphone or PC are paid together with the monthly mobile charges[8]. The bill goes to the person who holds the contract with the carrier, who must agree to the use[8]. Stripe's PayPay documentation lists a business location in Japan, customers in Japan and yen pricing, with payments from 50 yen to 1,000,000 yen[9].

KOMOJU describes Paidy as a "buy now, pay later" service that lets customers pay later without a credit card[4], and says Paidy is available without a local entity[11].

Which payment gateway or payment processor in Japan can an overseas shop use?

A payment gateway or payment processor is the service that connects your checkout to card networks, banks and other payment methods. For Japan, the question is which methods a provider can switch on for a business like yours. The two providers we checked answer it differently by method.

  • Stripe's documentation lists a business location in Japan for Konbini[3], PayPay[9] and yen bank transfers[5].
  • KOMOJU says konbini payment at five chains, Pay-easy, bank transfer, Paidy and WebMoney (for digital content) are available without a local entity, and that 7-Eleven konbini, Rakuten Pay, carrier billing and card processing through a Japanese bank need one. It adds that exemptions may apply depending on the business model and size[11].

Provider terms change, so confirm the current list with the provider before you plan a checkout around a method. The conditions can also depend on what you sell, because providers keep lists of prohibited and restricted business categories[3]. We have not compared providers on price or service, and this page does not recommend one.

Where does a yen stablecoin fit among Japan payment options?

A yen stablecoin is a digital token tied to the Japanese yen. It is another way to be paid by buyers in Japan, not a replacement for cards, konbini payment or the other methods above. It works only for buyers who can get the token, and it is not credit, so the buyer cannot pay later or in installments. Because a final blockchain transfer cannot be undone, refunds are sent back by the merchant. This guide gives no figures on how many buyers hold yen stablecoins. For the legal background and the issuer, read Accepting payments from customers in Japan with a yen stablecoin, the yen stablecoin guide and how to accept stablecoin payments.

How does STBLpay fit?

STBLpay (すてぶるペイ) is a payment service in which buyers pay in yen and merchants receive JPYC. It is under development, and the launch date has not been set (as of November 2026).

  • The buyer pays by Japanese-yen bank transfer and approves the payment with a passkey (face or fingerprint). If the buyer's JPYC balance is enough, no bank transfer is needed. Buyers need a JPYC EX account, which is available to individuals residing in Japan, so STBLpay is for selling to buyers in Japan.
  • The merchant receives sales in JPYC. The service does not rely on card companies' credit screening. STBLpay connects the merchant's and the buyer's wallets. It is a permissionless design with no step where the provider approves each merchant, so there is no card-style prior merchant screening.
  • The merchant withdraws to yen from its own JPYC EX account, which is available to individuals residing in Japan and Japanese corporations.
  • The provider, Ango-ya LLC, does not hold buyers' or merchants' funds or private keys (non-custodial).
  • The fee is under consideration. A single flat rate for all merchants is planned, and nothing is added on top for the buyer.
  • Use is subject to the terms of service, in which the merchant represents that its business is not illegal under Japanese law, and to legal requirements.

Frequently asked questions

What are the most common payment methods in Japan?

Credit cards make up most cashless spending. For 2025, Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) counted 162.7 trillion yen of cashless payments, of which credit cards were 134.6 trillion yen and code payments (QR codes and similar) were 16.6 trillion yen. Other methods used online include convenience-store (konbini) payment, bank transfer, cash on delivery, carrier billing and pay-later services.

Can an overseas online shop offer Japanese payment methods without a Japanese company?

It depends on the provider and the method. KOMOJU says konbini payment at five chains, Pay-easy, bank transfer, Paidy and WebMoney (for digital content) are available without a local entity, while 7-Eleven konbini, carrier billing and card processing through a Japanese bank need one. Stripe's documentation lists a business location in Japan for Konbini, PayPay and yen bank transfers. Check your provider's current terms.

How does konbini payment work for an online order?

The buyer receives a payment code and a confirmation number at checkout, takes them to a convenience store and pays in cash. The shop is told of the payment afterward, not at the moment of checkout. Our konbini guide covers the details.

What is atobarai (pay later) in Japan?

Atobarai means paying after the purchase. With a service such as Paidy, the buyer completes the order with a smartphone and pays by the 27th of the following month. KOMOJU describes Paidy as a buy now, pay later service that lets customers pay later without a credit card.

Can I use cash on delivery when shipping to Japan from overseas?

Japan Post says it does not provide cash on delivery for international mail. Its domestic cash-on-delivery service collects the amount from the recipient at delivery and sends it to an account at a financial institution in Japan. Check other carriers separately.

Can customers in Japan pay with a yen stablecoin instead?

STBLpay is a payment service in which buyers pay in yen and merchants receive JPYC. It is under development, and the launch date has not been set (as of November 2026). The buyer pays by Japanese-yen bank transfer and approves the payment with a passkey. If the buyer's JPYC balance is enough, no bank transfer is needed.
