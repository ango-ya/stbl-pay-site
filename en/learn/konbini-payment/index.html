What Is Konbini Payment? Convenience-Store Payments in Japan Explained

Konbini payment is a way for customers in Japan to pay for an online order in cash at a convenience store ("konbini" is the Japanese word for convenience store)[1]. The buyer gets a payment code at checkout, takes it to a store such as FamilyMart or Lawson, and pays there. The merchant is told once the cash is paid, so the order stays pending until then. This guide covers how a konbini payment works online, what the merchant gets, and the alternatives, including a yen stablecoin.

Updated: November 9, 2026　By Ango-ya LLC

What is konbini payment?

Konbini payment, also called the konbini payment method, lets customers in Japan pay for bills and online purchases at a convenience store with cash[1]. The buyer receives payment codes for specific store chains and a confirmation number, then brings them to a store to pay[1]. Because the buyer pays in cash, the payment does not create an entry on the buyer's bank or card statement[2].

Payment providers such as Stripe and KOMOJU offer it[1][3]. Details below come from those two providers' public documentation, and other providers may differ.

How does a konbini payment work online, step by step?

This is the buyer's path in Stripe's flow, with KOMOJU's differences noted.

  1. At checkout, the buyer selects konbini and enters a name and email address[1]. With KOMOJU, the buyer also picks the store chain where they plan to pay[4].
  2. The buyer receives payment instructions: payment codes, a confirmation number and an expiration time for each supported chain. They can view the instructions on desktop or mobile, or print them[1].
  3. Before the instructions expire, the buyer shows the code and confirmation number at a supported store in Japan and pays in cash[1].
  4. The store gives the buyer a receipt. The provider then confirms the payment and notifies the merchant, who can fulfill the order[1].

What does the merchant get from a konbini payment online?

The merchant gets a delayed confirmation. Stripe describes Konbini as a delayed notification method, because customers might not pay at the store right after checkout[2]. KOMOJU's payments stay in an "authorized" status while the system waits for the buyer to pay in cash, and a "captured" event arrives once they do[4]. In practice, hold shipping until the confirmation arrives.

Stripe's documentation lists these terms for Konbini. They are Stripe's terms as of November 2026, not a market standard.

Konbini terms in Stripe's documentation
Item What Stripe's documentation says
Stores FamilyMart, Lawson, Ministop and Seicomart stores across Japan[1]
Payment confirmation Delayed. The merchant is notified after the buyer pays at the store[1][2]
Payout Funds are available for payout after 4 business days[1]
Expiry Default is 3 days, ending at 23:59 Japan time. The merchant can set 1 to 60 days[2]
Amount 120 to 300,000 yen per payment[1]
Refunds Full and partial refunds are supported, sent by bank transfer after the buyer provides bank details[1][2]
Chargebacks Not supported[1]
Recurring payments Automatic recurring charges are not supported. The buyer pays each time, for example a subscription invoice[1]

Two details matter for expiry. After the deadline the buyer can no longer start the payment at a store kiosk, but a buyer who printed a valid payment slip before the deadline might still pay at the register afterward[2]. When a payment clearly did not complete, typically about an hour after the deadline, Stripe sends a failure event[2].

Providers differ. KOMOJU supports 7-Eleven, Family Mart, Lawson, Ministop, Seicomart and Daily Yamazaki, with a 1 yen minimum and 300,000 yen maximum[3]. KOMOJU also says the buyer pays a fixed processing fee at the store[3].

Can you refund a konbini payment?

Yes, but not to the original payment source. Stripe explains that konbini payments are made in cash, so it emails the buyer to collect bank account details and then sends the refund as a bank transfer[1]. If the buyer does not provide the details within 45 days, the refund fails and the merchant must refund the buyer outside Stripe[2]. Since there is no chargeback, a refund depends on the buyer replying to that email[1].

Can an overseas merchant use konbini payment?

It depends on the provider. Stripe's Konbini page lists Japan as the required business location, Japan as the customer location, Japanese yen as the currency, and domestic rather than cross-border coverage[1]. It also names business categories that cannot use Konbini, and says its financial partner and the store chains might decline a business at their discretion[1]. We did not confirm the rules of other providers for overseas merchants, so ask yours before you plan around it.

What are the pros and cons of konbini payment for merchants?

These points come from the provider documentation above.

Pros

  • The buyer does not need a card, because they pay in cash[1].
  • Customers cannot file chargebacks[1].
  • Several store chains across Japan accept the payment[1][3].

Cons

  • The payment is delayed. The order stays pending until the buyer pays, and the payment lapses if the buyer misses the deadline[2].
  • There is a cap of 300,000 yen per payment[1][3].
  • Refunds need the buyer to supply bank details, and they fail after 45 days without them[2].
  • It does not support automatic recurring charges, and Stripe's version is limited to Japan and yen[1].
  • The buyer pays a fixed fee at the store, according to KOMOJU[3].

What are the alternatives to konbini payment, including a yen stablecoin?

Other ways to get paid by buyers in Japan include a domestic bank transfer and a yen stablecoin. Each fits different orders.

How does STBLpay fit?

STBLpay (すてぶるペイ) is a payment service in which buyers pay in yen and merchants receive JPYC. It is under development, and the launch date has not been set (as of November 2026).

  • The buyer pays by Japanese-yen bank transfer and approves the payment with a passkey (face or fingerprint). If the buyer's JPYC balance is enough, no bank transfer is needed. Buyers need a JPYC EX account, which is available to individuals residing in Japan, so STBLpay is for selling to buyers in Japan.
  • The merchant receives sales in JPYC. The service does not rely on card companies' credit screening. STBLpay connects the merchant's and the buyer's wallets. It is a permissionless design with no step where the provider approves each merchant, so there is no card-style prior merchant screening.
  • The merchant withdraws to yen from its own JPYC EX account, which is available to individuals residing in Japan and Japanese corporations.
  • The provider, Ango-ya LLC, does not hold buyers' or merchants' funds or private keys (non-custodial).
  • The fee is under consideration. A single flat rate for all merchants is planned, and nothing is added on top for the buyer.
  • Use is subject to the terms of service, in which the merchant represents that its business is not illegal under Japanese law, and to legal requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Can a customer outside Japan use konbini payment?

Konbini payment is built for customers in Japan who pay at a store in Japan. Stripe's Konbini page lists Japan as the customer location, Japanese yen as the currency, and domestic coverage rather than cross-border. A buyer who is not in Japan cannot walk into one of those stores.

How long does a konbini payment stay open?

It depends on the setting you choose. In Stripe, the default is 3 days, ending at 23:59 Japan time, and the merchant can set 1 to 60 days. After the deadline the buyer can no longer start the payment at a store kiosk.

What is the maximum amount for a konbini payment?

Stripe lists 120 yen as the minimum and 300,000 yen as the maximum for a Konbini payment. KOMOJU lists 1 yen and 300,000 yen. Limits differ by provider, so check the one you use.

Can you refund a konbini payment?

Yes, but not to the original payment source, because the buyer paid in cash. In Stripe, the buyer is emailed to provide bank account details and the refund is sent as a bank transfer. If the buyer does not do so within 45 days, the refund fails and the merchant must refund by another method.

Is there a chargeback on konbini payments?

Stripe says Konbini does not support the standard dispute process, so customers cannot file chargebacks. In rare cases a store may report an irregularity, such as payment mishandling, and Stripe then contacts the merchant with next steps.

Is there a yen stablecoin alternative to konbini payment?

STBLpay is one option under development. The buyer pays by Japanese-yen bank transfer and approves with a passkey, and the merchant receives JPYC. The launch date has not been set (as of November 2026).
